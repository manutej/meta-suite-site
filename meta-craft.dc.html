meta-craft

A tailored craft constitution — plus an evidence-based Definition of Done.

// what it does

"Good" is not a feeling. meta-craft writes the constitution for a kind of work — the standards it must meet — and pairs it with a Definition of Done you can actually check against, not just nod at.

The standards are tailored to the task and grounded in evidence — so "done" means it cleared a bar, not that someone got tired of looking at it.

// the anatomy
constitution

The non-negotiable standards this kind of work must meet.

definition of done

A checkable bar — pass or fail, not vibes.

evidence

Why each standard is there — so it holds up under push-back.

Grounding

The ideas here aren't invented — the skill generalizes established practice into one reusable, task-agnostic move. Below: the prior art it builds on, and the in-repo contract it actually runs on.

Prior art
Definition of Done — the Scrum Guide (Schwaber & Sutherland) — completion is agreed in advance and shown with evidence, not asserted.
The Pragmatic Programmer — Hunt & Thomas (1999) — clear beats clever, reuse before building, validate at the boundaries.
Rule of Three — Martin Fowler, Refactoring — no abstraction until the repetition is real.
In this suite
The 13 craft principles references/principles.md — tailored to the 5–8 that bite for the work at hand — plus the evidence-based Definition of Done.
